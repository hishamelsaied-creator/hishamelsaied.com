Leadership transition check

Eighteen statements, about four minutes. For anyone in their first year as a director, head of function or general manager. You'll see where you're settling in well, and where to focus next.

AHow long have you been in the role?
BWhat kind of move was it?

For each statement, choose how true it is for you today. Some are worded negatively on purpose, so read each one.

1My manager and I have agreed what success looks like in my first year.
2I feel able to handle the hardest parts of this job.
3I've met each key stakeholder one to one.
4I've handed over the work that now belongs to someone else.
5I've formed a view of each person's strengths and gaps, based on evidence.
6I've picked one or two early wins that the business itself cares about.
7I know which decisions are mine to make and which I need to take to others.
8When something goes wrong, I know who to call and what to do.
9I understand how decisions are really made here, beyond the org chart.
10Most of my week goes on work that only someone at my level can do.
11My team knows what I expect and how we'll work together.
12People know what I'm focusing on, and why.
13I'm still unsure what my manager expects me to deliver first.
14I often feel out of my depth in this role.
15There are senior people whose support I need but don't yet have.
16I still step in to do work my team should be doing.
17I've put off a people decision I know needs making.
18I've started big changes before fully understanding how things work here.
The research behind this tool

The six areas come from three well-established bodies of work on leaders in new roles.

  • Newcomer adjustment. A meta-analysis of 70 samples found that role clarity, self-efficacy (confidence you can do the job) and social acceptance carry the effect of onboarding through to satisfaction, commitment, performance and staying (Bauer et al., 2007). A 2025 update again highlighted social acceptance. These give the first three areas.
  • Leadership passages. Each step up asks leaders to change their skills, how they spend their time and what they value. Holding on to the old job is one of the most common reasons transitions stall (Charan, Drotter and Noel, The Leadership Pipeline).
  • The first 90 days. Securing early wins, building the team and matching your approach to the situation you've inherited (Watkins, The First 90 Days).

How it's scored. Each area has three statements, one worded negatively and reverse-scored to reduce the pull to agree with everything. Area scores run from 0 to 100. The tool also checks whether your answers within an area point in different directions, and reads your scores against how long you've been in the role.

What it isn't. This is a structured reflection, not a validated psychometric test, and it hasn't been normed on a population. Use it to decide where to look, then check your view with your manager or a coach.

Sources: Bauer, T. N., Bodner, T., Erdogan, B., Truxillo, D. M., & Tucker, J. S. (2007). Newcomer adjustment during organizational socialization. Journal of Applied Psychology, 92(3), 707–721. · Bauer, T. N. et al. (2025). New horizons for newcomer organizational socialization: A review, meta-analysis, and future research directions. Journal of Management, 51(1), 344–382. · Charan, R., Drotter, S., & Noel, J. The Leadership Pipeline. · Watkins, M. D. The First 90 Days. Harvard Business Review Press.
