Will AI coaching replace human coaches? An honest answer from a coach

I'm building an AI version of myself. So I've had to think hard about what it can and can't do.

By Hisham Elsaied, executive coach and author, Dubai  ·  November 2026

I'll declare an interest first. I'm a human coach, and I'm also building Ask Hisham, an AI coach trained on my own method and voice. So I have reasons to believe both sides. Here is what the evidence says.

What the research found

In 2022, researchers led by Nicky Terblanche published a comparison of two randomised controlled trials run over ten months. One tested human coaches, the other an AI chatbot coach. Both helped people reach their goals more than doing nothing, and on that single measure the AI coach did about as well as the humans. The authors were careful to say this was a narrow, structured task.

In 2026 a team including the same researchers ran the first direct head-to-head trial with 114 participants, comparing accredited human coaches with AI coaches on goals, wellbeing and self-efficacy, alongside a group that received no coaching. This time human coaching came out clearly ahead.

Put simply: when the job is to move someone through a clear goal, step by step, AI can help. When the job is to be the coach, people still do it better.

Where AI genuinely helps

  • Between sessions. Reflecting on a meeting the same evening, preparing for a hard conversation at midnight, keeping a commitment alive.
  • Access. Many people who could use coaching will never pay for a human coach. A good AI coach can give them a real start.
  • Structure. AI is consistent. It doesn't forget the goal you set three weeks ago.

Where it falls short

Even the International Coaching Federation's AI Coaching Framework and Standards, published in 2024, accepts that real presence and a trusting, safe relationship may be harder for an AI coach to create. In my experience, the moments that change a leader are rarely in the words. They're in what the person almost said, the pause before an answer, the thing they keep coming back to. AI is getting better at noticing patterns in text. It doesn't sit with you in a difficult silence.

There is also responsibility. If a coaching conversation touches on burnout, a serious conflict or a decision that affects someone's family, a human needs to be in the room.

How I'm handling it with Ask Hisham

That's why Ask Hisham will always say clearly that it's AI, be clear about who can see what you share, and tell people when something needs a real session. I see it as a way for more people to start, not a replacement for the work two people do together.

If you're choosing

  1. For a clear, structured goal and regular practice, AI coaching can be a good, low-cost start.
  2. For a career decision, a hard relationship or a move to a bigger role, choose a credentialed human coach.
  3. Whichever you use, ask who sees your data and what happens when the tool is out of its depth.
