The research behind this tool

Feedback doesn't always help. In the largest review of the research, covering 607 effects and more than 23,000 observations, feedback improved performance on average, but in over a third of cases it made performance worse (Kluger and DeNisi, 1996). Their explanation: feedback works when it keeps attention on the task, and backfires when it pulls attention onto the person, their worth or how they compare with others.

That's why the builder uses the Situation, Behaviour, Impact structure developed by the Center for Creative Leadership, adds a question to hear the other person's view, and ends with one forward-looking request. The quality check looks for five things: a specific moment, behaviour rather than a label, a stated impact, a request someone could actually act on, and timing. It also asks how you feel, because feedback given in anger is more likely to land on the person than the task.

In much of the Gulf, how something is said matters as much as what is said. Keep corrective feedback private, and leave the person a way to keep face.

Sources: Kluger, A. N., & DeNisi, A. (1996). The effects of feedback interventions on performance: A historical review, a meta-analysis, and a preliminary feedback intervention theory. Psychological Bulletin, 119(2), 254–284. · Center for Creative Leadership, Situation-Behavior-Impact (SBI) model.
