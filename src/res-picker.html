Coaching, mentoring or training?

Eight questions, about three minutes. Answer for yourself, or for a leader in your team.

1What best describes the gap?
2Do others in the team have the same gap?
3What would help most right now?
4What is the issue mostly about?
5Where is the answer?
6How much time is there?
7How ready is the person to work on this?
8Afterwards, will their manager give them time and support to use what they learn?
The research behind this tool

Coaching, mentoring and training solve different problems. Training builds a known skill. Mentoring passes on someone's experience of a world. Coaching helps a person work out their own answer to a problem that depends on them. The questions look at the type of gap, who shares it, where the answer sits, and the time frame.

How it's scored. Questions 1 and 5 count double, because the kind of gap and where the answer lies are the most telling. The gap between the top two scores gives a clear, leaning or mixed result. Questions 7 and 8 don't change the recommendation. They check two conditions the research links to whether any of the three works: the person's readiness, and support at work afterwards.
