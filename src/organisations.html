For HR and organisations

Coaching for your leaders, set up so you can see the result.

If you're in HR or you sponsor a leader's development, this page explains how I work with organisations: how we agree goals, what you will and won't hear, and how we check what changed.

Talk about a leader
When organisations call me

The situations I'm brought in for most.

New in role

A leader has just stepped up.

A new director, a first head of HR, a specialist moving into a business partner role, or a national promoted into a senior seat. The first months shape how they're seen, and coaching shortens them.

Strong, but stuck

Good at the job, not yet seen as a leader.

Trusted to deliver but not brought into decisions, quiet in senior meetings, or struggling to influence peers. Usually a question of presence and influence, not ability.

Succession

Getting a successor ready.

Someone named for a bigger role in the next year or two who needs to close specific gaps before the move.

HR capability

An HR team that wants a bigger seat.

Coaching for the HR head alongside work with the team, so HR is brought in earlier and listened to more.

How an engagement runs

Six steps, usually over about three months.

  1. Brief

    You tell me about the leader, the role and what the organisation needs to see. Thirty minutes is enough.

  2. Chemistry meeting

    I meet the leader for a short conversation with no commitment. They decide whether I'm the right coach. If I'm not, I'll say so.

  3. Three-way goal setting

    The leader, their manager and I agree two or three specific goals, and what people around the leader should notice when they're met.

  4. Sessions

    Regular one-to-one sessions, in person in the UAE or online across the GCC, in Arabic or English. Work is built on real situations from the leader's week.

  5. Midpoint review

    A short three-way check on progress. If something needs to change, this is where we change it.

  6. Final review

    We look at the goals again with the manager, and with a few colleagues where agreed, and decide what the leader keeps working on after we finish.

Confidentiality

What you'll hear, and what you won't.

Coaching only works if the leader can speak freely. It's also fair that whoever pays knows the work is happening. I agree this split at the start, in writing, with everyone involved.

You will hear

Goals, attendance and progress.

The goals agreed in the three-way meeting, whether sessions are taking place, and the progress discussed openly at the midpoint and final reviews.

You won't hear

What's said in the sessions.

The content of our conversations stays between the leader and me, in line with the ICF Code of Ethics. The only exceptions are the ones the Code sets out, such as a risk of serious harm or an illegal act.

Measuring the change

How we check that something actually changed.

Before and after

The people who work with the leader.

At the start and the end, the manager and a few colleagues rate the leader on the agreed goals, in a few minutes each. The comparison shows whether others noticed the change, not only whether the leader feels better.

Assessment

Hogan or a 360 where it helps.

For some leaders a Hogan assessment or a 360 at the start gives a clearer picture of how they come across under normal conditions and under pressure. I'm a Hogan Certified Assessor.

If coaching stalls, for example sessions keep being moved or the leader isn't engaging, I tell the sponsor early. Then we either reset the goals or stop. Nobody benefits from sessions that aren't working.

Questions

What HR teams ask before they start.

How long does an engagement last?

Usually about three months, which is long enough for others to notice a change in behaviour when the goals are clear. Some leaders continue for longer. We plan around Ramadan and the summer.

Can the coaching be in Arabic?

Yes. I coach in Arabic and English, and many leaders move between the two in the same session.

Do you coach in person?

In person in the UAE, and online or in person across the GCC. Most engagements mix the two.

Can we meet you before committing?

Yes. You can brief me first, and the leader always has a chemistry meeting before anything is agreed.

How are fees set?

Per engagement, not per hour, based on length, format and any assessment. I send a short written proposal after the brief.

Can you coach several leaders at once?

Yes. For a group, I'd agree shared themes with you at the start and report on themes across the group, never on what an individual said.
