Agree and plan

Make it happen

Three if-then plans

For example: “When the Sunday leadership meeting ends, I'll spend ten minutes writing what I heard.” In controlled studies, plans written this way made people noticeably more likely to follow through.
Watch for

Three common traps

  • Copying what worked in your last organisation without asking.
  • Becoming seen as one person's messenger too fast.
  • Disappearing into day-to-day operations.
The research behind this tool

Situation first. Michael Watkins found that new leaders often fail by using the playbook from their last job. His STARS model names five situations a leader can inherit, start-up, turnaround, accelerated growth, realignment and sustaining success, each needing a different start. The planner adjusts its advice to the one you choose.

Listen, then act. The three phases follow the same research: learn how things really work, secure early wins the business values, then agree the bigger plan with your manager.

If-then plans. A meta-analysis of 94 tests found that linking a specific cue to a specific action had a medium-to-large effect on reaching goals (d = 0.65; Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006). A much larger 2024 review found a smaller but still reliable effect on behaviour, so treat them as a strong habit, not magic.
